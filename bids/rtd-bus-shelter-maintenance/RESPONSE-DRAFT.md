#### 3.2 Graffiti Removal Plan (SOW §6, p.6)

**Standard.** SOW §6: "Graffiti shall be removed upon discovery from shelters, trash receptacles, and concrete surfaces maintained
under this specification." "In no instance shall graffiti remain for more than 72 hours unless written approval is received from
RTD FM Manager." The Contract's deduction is tighter: "$200.00 per occurrence for each graffiti tag that is not removed during the
daily servicing of the RTD property. This deduction can be assessed every eight hours" (FoC Deduction Schedule, p.49). **Brown
Bison's standard: every tag is removed or covered at the service visit where it is found.** A tag that needs a paint match or a
second pass is finished the same day. Etched glass or polycarbonate that cannot be restored is photographed and reported to RTD the
same day, and Brown Bison asks RTD's FM Manager for written direction on restoring it; SOW §6 requires "the surface restored to
its pre graffiti condition" and allows graffiti to remain more than 72 hours only with "written approval ... from RTD FM Manager".

**A. Equipment.** On every route truck: the hot-water pressure washer (minimum 3000 PSI) with a surface cleaner and adjustable
tips; RTD-approved graffiti removers (one for porous masonry, one labeled safe for polycarbonate); new razor blades and blade
holders for glass; plastic scrapers and non-scratch pads for polycarbonate; microfiber; a poultice for stains in porous concrete;
color-matched exterior paint, primer, brushes, rollers and tape; absorbent socks and drain covers; nitrile gloves, eye protection
and respirators as the Safety Data Sheets require. [SUB-C: product names and SDS for RTD Safety approval.]

**B. Training.** Before working alone, each technician completes: Safety Data Sheet and hazard-communication training on every
product; PPE; identifying the surface before choosing a method; the test-patch rule (try the mildest method on a small hidden area
first); the polycarbonate do-not-use list below; storm-drain protection; color matching and painting from a parting line; and
before-and-after photos in MapToTrack. The supervisor signs off the first five removals on each surface type. Records are kept
and available to RTD (SOW §2.1.A, p.1: "A copy of this training program must be submitted to RTD upon request").

**C. Techniques by surface.** SOW §6: "When removing graffiti on concrete, removal, rather than cover, is the first priority and
the preferred method."

| Surface | Method |
|---|---|
| Concrete (pads, walks, curbs) | Apply masonry graffiti remover, dwell per label, agitate, rinse with hot water at the lowest pressure that lifts it, using a surface cleaner to avoid wand marks. Poultice for shadows left in porous concrete. Cover with matching paint only when removal fails, and paint in a neat rectangle to the nearest joint. |
| Concrete block | Same as concrete; block is more porous, so lower pressure and a second application rather than more pressure. Paint-over uses matched color on whole block faces. |
| Glass | Paint and marker: wet the glass, lift with a new razor blade held flat at a low angle, then glass cleaner. Never use a worn blade or dry scraping. Stickers: steam or adhesive remover, then blade. Light scratching: glass polishing compound by hand pad. |
| Polycarbonate (schedule boards and any clear panels) | Mild detergent and warm water first, then a remover labeled safe for polycarbonate; microfiber or non-scratch pad only; rinse well. **Never** razor blades, abrasive pads, acetone, lacquer thinner, MEK, toluene or other strong solvents, and no high pressure close to the panel: they cloud, craze or scratch it. |
| Painted steel and trash receptacles | Remover safe for the finish; if the coating is damaged, spot-prime and repaint to match. |

**D. Glass and polycarbonate restoration in detail** (SOW §6.D: "particularly challenging. Provide detailed information").
1. Identify the panel: glass is cold and rigid, does not flex, and sounds hard when tapped; polycarbonate is warmer and slightly
   flexible. When unsure, treat it as polycarbonate.
2. Test patch in a corner. If the remover softens the panel surface or leaves haze, stop and switch to detergent only.
3. Glass: surface ink and paint come off with blade and cleaner. Acid etching or deep scratches are permanent; light scratches are
   polished. Anything left is photographed and reported to RTD with a recommendation (polish, film or replacement).
4. Polycarbonate: work from the mildest method up; rinse every chemical off within its dwell time; dry with microfiber. Etching
   and scratches cannot be removed without damaging the panel, so they are reported to RTD for direction rather than polished.
5. Restore the look: the panel is clean, streak-free and evenly clear, with no ghost of the tag. If a ghost remains, the shelter
   is reported, not left as "done".
6. Close-up before-and-after photos, time and date stamped, go into MapToTrack with the stop number.

**Paint matching.** SOW §6: "When painting over graffiti, paint should match as close as industry color matching will allow. On

and sidewalk. No lane closure is planned.

  or the bus loading zone. Strobes on whenever stopped. Where the only way to reach a stop would put the vehicle in a travel lane,

  the local standard, set up by a traffic-control firm with certified flaggers and devices, permitted before work starts.

**Pedestrians.** An accessible path past the work is kept open at all times. Curb ramps, crosswalk landings and the bus boarding

**Bicycles.** No vehicle, hose, cone line or snow pile in a bike lane. Spray and blower discharge point away from bike lanes.

**Emergency vehicles.** Crews yield and move equipment for any emergency vehicle. Hydrants, fire lanes and transfer-center
driveways stay clear; no hoses across them; no snow piled on or against hydrants.
