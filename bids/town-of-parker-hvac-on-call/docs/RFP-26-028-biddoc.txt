1. Solicitation Overview
The Town of Parker, Colorado (Town) is soliciting proposals from qualified commercial
HVAC contractors to provide scheduled preventive maintenance, seasonal inspections,
testing, documentation, emergency response, and corrective repair services for heating,
cooling, ventilation, chiller, and pool dehumidification equipment at Town facilities. The
current inventory includes 46 assets: 40 rooftop units, four DX chillers, and two pool
dehumidification units. The objective is to improve reliability, safety, energy
performance, service continuity, and equipment life while minimizing disruption to Town
operations.

3. Contract Framework
3.1 Anticipated Agreement and Term
The selected contractor will be required to execute a written agreement acceptable to
the Town. The anticipated initial term is three years. The Town may exercise renewal
options in one-year increments, subject to satisfactory performance, available
appropriations, mutual agreement, and the terms of the resulting agreement. No work
may begin until the agreement is fully executed and the Town issues written
authorization or a purchase order, as applicable.

3.2 Pricing and Changes
Pricing shall remain firm for the period stated in the proposal and resulting agreement.
Any requested adjustment for a renewal period must be submitted in writing with
supporting documentation and is subject to Town approval. No surcharge, travel fee,
fuel fee, environmental fee, disposal fee, refrigerant fee, or other cost will be paid
unless specifically identified in the accepted pricing and authorized in writing.

3.3 Contractor Status and Compliance
The contractor will act as an independent contractor and shall provide all labor,
supervision, tools, vehicles, equipment, access equipment, testing instruments, permits,
licenses, safety controls, and incident reporting necessary to perform the work. The
contractor shall comply with applicable federal, state, and local laws, codes,
regulations, manufacturer requirements, and site rules.

3.4 Insurance Security and Background Requirements
Before commencing work, the selected contractor shall provide certificates of insurance
and endorsements meeting the Town’s then-current requirements and comply with the
indemnification provisions of the final agreement. Personnel assigned to secure or
sensitive facilities may be subject to identification requirements, escorts, access
controls, or background screening established by the Town.

5. Scope of Services & Other Requirements
5.1 General Requirements
The contractor shall furnish all labor, supervision, tools, testing instruments, equipment,
transportation, reports, and routine consumables necessary to perform scheduled and
unscheduled HVAC service. Work shall be performed by qualified commercial HVAC
technicians familiar with the makes, models, controls, refrigerants, and configurations in
Appendix A.
  • Coordinate all visits with the Town’s designated representative and comply with
    site access, security, roof access, shutdown, noise, and restoration requirements.
  • Schedule work to minimize disruption to occupied facilities, public safety
    operations, recreation programming, cultural events, and other Town services.
  • Protect roofs, finishes, equipment, occupants, and Town property. Immediately
    report unsafe conditions, leaks, spills, damage, alarms, or equipment conditions
    that could impair operation.
  • Maintain clean work areas and legally manage refrigerants, oils, cleaning
    solutions, contaminated materials, and other service waste.
  • Do not disable, bypass, shut down, restart, or return equipment to service without
    required coordination and authorization.
  • Use calibrated instruments and follow manufacturer instructions and applicable
    codes. ANSI ASHRAE ACCA Standard 180 may be used as reference guidance
    where appropriate but is not incorporated as a mandatory contractual requirement.
 •​
5.2 Initial Verification and Baseline Record
Within 30 calendar days after notice to proceed, unless another timeframe is approved
in writing, the contractor shall verify the inventory and submit a baseline electronic
record for each asset. At minimum, the record shall include:
  • Facility, equipment identifier, exact location, access requirements, and applicable
    service season.
  • Equipment type, manufacturer, model, serial number, date or approximate age,
    capacity, voltage, phase, refrigerant type and charge when available, belts, and
    associated controls.
  • Apparent condition, active alarms, operational status, photographs, identified
    deficiencies, and recommended priority.
  • Corrections to Appendix A and any information that could not be verified.

5.3 Annual Schedule and Service Frequency
The contractor shall submit a coordinated annual schedule for all covered assets.
Cooling-season service is anticipated during March or April, and heating-season service
is anticipated during September for the assets marked in Appendix A. The final
schedule may be adjusted by the Town to reflect weather, facility operations, equipment
replacement, and event calendars. The schedule shall identify the proposed date,
equipment, estimated duration, staffing, access needs, expected shutdowns, and any
work outside normal hours.

5.4 Cooling Season Preventive Maintenance
Perform the following tasks on each applicable asset, as dictated by equipment type,
configuration, and manufacturer requirements:
 • Inspect and clean accessible evaporator and condenser coils on rooftop units
   using methods and cleaning agents compatible with the equipment, roof system,
   occupants, and surrounding materials. Protect drains and prevent chemical
   discharge or overspray.



                                             8
 • Inspect condensate pans, traps, drains, pumps, and lines; clear obstructions,
   prime traps, and verify proper drainage without leaks or overflow.
 • Visually inspect for refrigerant leaks. Check refrigerant pressures, superheat, and
   subcooling when conditions permit and when readings are necessary to evaluate
   operation. Document refrigerant additions and leak findings.
 • Inspect disconnects, fuses, contactors, relays, wiring, and electrical components.
   Tighten accessible connections in accordance with manufacturer requirements.
 • Measure and record voltage and amperage for applicable motors and
   compressors; compare readings with nameplate and expected operating
   conditions.
 • Inspect belts, blowers, sheaves, pulleys, bearings, motors, vibration, alignment,
   and tension. Lubricate motors and bearings only where required by the
   manufacturer.
 • Record return-air and supply-air temperatures and calculate temperature
   differential. When readings are abnormal, perform appropriate diagnostics and
   document findings.
 • Inspect economizers, outdoor-air dampers, actuators, valves, and linkages; verify
   proper operation and positioning.
 • Verify thermostat, sensor, alarm, safety, and BAS setpoint operation. Document
   any changes and obtain authorization before altering control sequences or
   occupied setpoints.
 • Secure all panels, remove service materials, confirm normal operation, and leave
   the unit in the proper operating mode.
 •​
5.5 Heating Season Preventive Maintenance
Perform the following tasks on every asset marked for heating service, as applicable to
its heating source and configuration:
  • Inspect gas piping, fittings, valves, regulators, and accessible connections for
    leaks using appropriate instruments or approved leak-detection methods.
  • Measure and document inlet and manifold gas pressure when applicable.
  • Inspect and clean burners and pilot assemblies. Inspect ignition systems, flame
    sensors, thermocouples, combustion motors, venting, and related safeties.
  • Inspect heat exchangers for visible cracks, corrosion, separation, flame
    disturbance, or other indications of failure. Immediately report any condition that
    may present a carbon-monoxide or combustion-safety risk.
  • Perform combustion analysis and carbon-monoxide testing on applicable fuel-
    fired equipment and document oxygen, carbon dioxide, carbon monoxide, stack
    temperature, draft, combustion efficiency, and other available readings.
  • Inspect electrical wiring and connections; measure and record voltage and
    amperage for applicable motors.
  • Inspect blowers, belts, bearings, motors, pulleys, alignment, and tension.
    Lubricate only where required by the manufacturer.
  • Verify thermostats, sensors, alarms, safeties, and BAS setpoints. Secure panels
    and confirm proper heating operation before leaving the site.

5.6 Chiller Hydronic and Pool Dehumidification Equipment
For DX chillers, pool dehumidification units, and associated hydronic components
included in the inventory, perform the applicable seasonal tasks above plus equipment-
specific maintenance recommended by the manufacturer. At minimum, the contractor
shall:
  • Inspect compressors, refrigerant circuits, fans, coils, safeties, crankcase heaters,
    controls, alarms, and staged operation.




                                            9
 • Inspect pumps, seals, couplings, strainers, expansion tanks, valves, actuators,
   flow devices, accessible piping, and insulation for leaks, corrosion, vibration,
   blockage, or abnormal operation.
 • Record applicable entering and leaving fluid or air temperatures, pressures,
   temperature differential, flow indication, refrigerant readings, electrical readings,
   and controller status.
 • Coordinate specialized inspection or manufacturer support when required and
   clearly identify work excluded from routine pricing.

5.7 Belts Lubricants and Routine Consumables
Offerors shall identify all belts, lubricants, cleaners, drain-treatment materials, gaskets,
fuses, and other routine consumables included in the preventive-maintenance price.
Products shall meet or exceed manufacturer requirements and be compatible with the
equipment. Reports shall document part numbers, sizes, quantities, and parts used.
Non-routine parts require Town authorization before installation unless immediate action
is expressly authorized to protect life, safety, or property.

5.8 Refrigerant Management
The contractor shall comply with applicable refrigerant-management requirements and
use appropriately certified technicians. Refrigerant shall not be added solely to restore
operation without evaluating the cause of loss. The contractor shall document
refrigerant type, quantity recovered or added, leak-detection findings, repair
recommendations, cylinder tracking, and required records. Recovery, recycling,
reclamation, and disposal shall be performed legally and safely.

5.9 Emergency and Corrective Repair Services
Provide 24-hour, seven-day-per-week emergency response, including after-hours,
weekends, and holidays. The contractor shall state and honor a guaranteed response
time, maintain qualified staffing and diagnostic resources, and prioritize critical public
safety and occupied facilities.
  • Notify the Town promptly of any condition that prevents reliable operation,
    presents a safety risk, or requires an extended outage.
  • Provide a written estimate and obtain Town authorization before non-routine
    repairs, parts replacement, or work exceeding an approved limit, except when
    immediate action is expressly authorized to protect life, safety, or property.
  • Provide new, genuine, or manufacturer-approved parts unless an alternative is
    authorized. Identify expected lead times, warranties, and temporary measures
    when parts are delayed.
  • Return equipment to its proper operating state, remove temporary connections,
    confirm alarms and setpoints, and verify status with the Town before leaving.

5.10 Reporting and Asset History
Submit searchable digital reports to the Town’s designated representative within five
business days after each scheduled visit and within one business day after an
emergency call, unless otherwise approved. Reports shall be organized by facility and
asset and include:
 • Date, arrival and departure times, technician, equipment identification, reason for
   service, and operating status when work concluded.
 • Completed checklist, work performed, consumables used, readings, setpoints,
   alarms, photographs, and applicable combustion and carbon-monoxide results.
 • Clear deficiency descriptions, severity or priority, immediate actions,
   recommended corrective work, parts required, estimated urgency, and follow-up
   responsibility.



                                             10
 • Maintenance history sufficient to track recurring failures, trends, repair decisions,
   and continuity across the contract term.

Any finding that could impair safe operation or materially affect facility service shall be
communicated immediately by telephone or another approved real-time method and
shall not wait for the written report.

5.11 Quality Control and Callback Work
The contractor shall review service reports for completeness and technical accuracy
before submission. Work that is incomplete, incorrectly performed, or results in a
callback attributable to contractor workmanship shall be corrected promptly at no
additional cost to the Town. The contractor shall identify its escalation process for
unresolved or recurring issues.

5.12 Warranty
The contractor shall warrant parts and workmanship for the period stated in its proposal
and the resulting agreement. Warranty service shall include diagnosis, labor, travel,
removal, replacement, and reinstallation necessary to correct covered defects, unless
an exclusion is expressly accepted by the Town. Manufacturer warranties shall be
passed through to the Town and administered by the contractor.

circuits, and controls.
