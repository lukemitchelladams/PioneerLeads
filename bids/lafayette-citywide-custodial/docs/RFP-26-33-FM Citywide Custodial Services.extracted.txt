## SECTION 1 – GENERAL INFORMATION
## BACKGROUND, OVERVIEW, AND GOALS
## Statement of Vision
Lafayette’s panoramic view of the Rocky Mountains inspires our view into the future. We value our heritage, our unique neighborhoods, a vibrant economy, and active lifestyles. We envision a future that mixes small town livability with balanced growth and superior city services.
## Statement of Values
We foresee a strong economy that is diverse and sustainable, attracts innovators, encourages a balance of big and small businesses, and meets the community’s needs.
We intend to shape the future through strengthening our voice on environmental stewardship and social issues, taking an active role in sustainability and strategic planning development, and facilitating leadership development and fresh thinking.
We strive to be a connected community that encourages cooperative relationships and inclusivity, expects accessibility and communication, nurtures resiliency, appreciates multiculturalism, and humanizes physical and social interactions within the City.
We support placemaking endeavors that stimulate historic preservation and the arts, encourage open space stewardship and networking of neighborhoods, and promote comprehensive planning and livability.
## PURPOSE
The City of Lafayette is seeking proposals from qualified firms to provide Citywide Janitorial Services at multiple facilities. The required result is to maintain the facility(s) in such a manner as to provide a clean, healthy, and safe work environment for occupants and visitors of	 the City of Lafayette building(s).

## Official Means of Communication
The RFP is published on the Rocky Mountain E-Purchasing System (Bidnet), so that proposals may be uploaded electronically to that system. The uploaded proposals are sealed by the system and can’t be viewed by the Procurement department or the evaluators until the due date and time have passed. The Procurement Manager will post notices that will include, but are not limited to, modifications to administrative or performance requirements, answers to inquiries received, clarifications to requirements, and the announcement of the apparent winning vendor.

## SECTION 3 – EVALUATION METHODOLOGY & AWARD
##  EVALUATION PROCESS
A comprehensive, thorough, complete, and impartial evaluation of each proposal received will be conducted. The award shall be made to the responsible Offeror whose proposal is determined to be the most advantageous to the City, taking into consideration the price and evaluation factors set forth in this Request for Proposal.
## EVALUATION COMMITTEE
An Evaluation Committee will be established utilizing measures to ensure the integrity of the evaluation process. These measures include the following:
Selecting committee members who do not have a conflict of interest regarding this solicitation.
Facilitating the independent review of proposals.
Requiring the evaluation of the proposals to be based strictly on the content of the proposals.
Ensuring the fair and impartial treatment of all Offerors. 
The objective of the Evaluation Committee is to conduct reviews of the proposals that have been submitted, to hold frank and detailed discussions among themselves, and to recommend an Offeror for award.
## PROPOSAL EVALUATION CRITERIA
The City will evaluate proposals to determine if each Offeror meets all mandatory qualification requirements; provided, however, that the City has the authority to waive non-material mandatory requirements in certain circumstances. The mandatory qualification requirements are scored on a Met/Not Met basis and only those proposals found to meet all mandatory requirements, other than non-material mandatory requirements waived by the City, can be considered for a Contract resulting from this solicitation.
Proposals will be evaluated by the Evaluation Committee using the evaluation criteria listed below. The evaluators will consider whether the Scope of Work requirements in the solicitation have been addressed, and they will review the capabilities of the Offeror, as well as the quality of the approach proposed, the price, and any other aspect determined relevant to the evaluation criteria. 
The evaluation criteria to be used in evaluating the proposals are as follows (and are listed in no particular order): 
[TABLE]
1. Pricing and Cost Reasonableness (35 points) / Evaluates the completeness, clarity, and reasonableness of the fixed-fee pricing submitted on Appendix C, Price Sheet and Master Cleaning Schedule, for every listed facility and service frequency. Pricing must include sufficient day porter hours for each listed operation, approximately one hour per week for work order system administration, and approximately one hour per month for supply ordering through the procurement portal designated by the City. Pricing must also include all equipment, chemicals, consumables, and personal protective equipment. Proposals with labor hours too low to perform the Master Cleaning Schedule, or with excluded or unpriced line items, will score lower. | 35 points
2. Staffing, Supervision, and Security Compliance (25 points) / Evaluates the proposed staffing levels and their alignment with service hours. This includes after-hours cleaning at non-secure buildings and escorted or controlled-access cleaning during operating hours at the Golf facility and the Police Station. Evaluators will consider the vendor's process for conducting security background checks and submitting employees for approval by the City before they work on-site. Also considered are employee training on confidentiality, key control, and access control, and the qualifications of the janitorial supervisor present during all service hours. The vendor's plan for daytime supervisor availability, monthly communication with the City, 24-hour issue notification, and coverage of staff absences and turnover will also be evaluated. | 25 points
3. Technical Approach, Quality Control, and Work Plan (20 points) / Evaluates the vendor's plan for performing routine custodial tasks, day porter duties, and periodic services at the required frequencies. Periodic services include floor scrubbing, stripping, finishing, and touch-up, and carpet cleaning using hot water extraction, bonnet, or rotary shampoo methods. Evaluators will consider the proposed equipment, which must be suitable for the work, low-noise, and operable on existing power. They will also consider the proposed non-flammable, non-harmful cleaning products and the safety practices, including wet floor signage, barricades, safety data sheet compliance, and OSHA 1910.132 personal protective equipment. The vendor's internal inspection and quality control process for meeting the stated cleanliness standards will be evaluated. So will its approach to responding to, completing, and closing out deficiencies and work requests in the work order system of the City within required timeframes. | 20 points
4. Experience, Past Performance, and References (20 points) / Evaluates the vendor's demonstrated experience providing comparable custodial services across multiple municipal or public sector facilities. Experience with secure facilities such as police stations, day porter programs, and periodic floor and carpet care is especially relevant. Evaluators will also consider the vendor's history of contract retention and at least three verifiable references for contracts of similar size and complexity. | 20 points
