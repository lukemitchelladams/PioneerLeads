## Tab 1. Cost Effectiveness & Pricing (p.21, IV-3.1)

### 1.1 How we help DEN meet the project budget without compromising quality

1. **One visit, one lockout.** Appendix I item 8 puts the coil inspection on every annual and semi-annual filter work order
   and the cleaning "prior to installing new air filters". We schedule each unit's coil cleaning on that visit, so DEN's HVAC
   team applies LOTO once and the unit is opened once. Our coil unit price assumes this pairing.
2. **Routing by building and level.** Each monthly batch is sequenced by building, level and mechanical room, with outlying
   buildings grouped by area of the campus. Less walking and driving between units is the largest cost we control.
3. **Level monthly loading.** A stable crew of trained, badged people is cheaper than peaks covered by overtime or new hires
   who are not yet badged. We split quarterly units into three monthly groups (Tab 4.3).
4. **Right filters, first trip.** Appendix III has 198 assets with no filter type and 98 filter sizes in mixed notation. In
   the first two monthly cycles we record the actual filter sizes, counts and depths at every unit and give DEN a corrected
   list. DEN's filter provider can then ship exact quantities, which cuts return trips, mis-sized stock and DEN's filter
   waste.
5. **Data for interval decisions.** We record pre- and post-change magnehelic readings where gauges exist (Exh A 4(1)(j)).
   Each quarter we give the Project Manager a short list of units whose pressure drop suggests a longer or shorter interval.
   The PM "may alter this schedule" (Appendix II); readings let DEN spend filters and labor where they are needed.
6. **No add-on charges.** The Pricing Form says vehicle cost, fuel, parking, trip charges, mobilization, subsistence, per
   diem, travel, insurance premiums, administrative costs, equipment and materials "will not be allowed". All of these are
   inside our unit prices. Work outside the price sheet is quoted in writing first (Exh A 3(4)).
7. **Fewer deficiencies.** Supervisor spot checks on a share of each night's units and a 24-hour correction rule (Tab 4.11)
   keep rework and CDF costs off the contract.

### 1.2 How we calculated our cost per unit and per service

We built each unit price from the documents, line by line, for the ten priced equipment types:

1. **Task content per unit type.** Appendix I items 1-11 broken into specialist and laborer minutes: LOTO request and
   verification with the DEN HVAC team, access, pre- and post-service photos and magnehelic readings, filter removal and
   installation, dating, HEPA vacuuming of the filter bank, frames and plenum, panels and clips, zero-bypass check, coil
   inspection, work tracking entry, clean-up and floor drains. The General Laborer handles filters, ladders and clean-up and
   does not touch HVAC equipment (Exh A 9(1)(d)(i)).
2. **Filter count per unit type** from Appendix III (pieces per service, with 6-inch final filters timed separately from
   2-inch pre-filters), applied to the Appendix II frequencies.
3. **Non-productive time** for movement inside the terminal, concourses and tunnels, drives to outlying buildings, escorts,
   daily Keywatcher key check-out and return, and end-of-shift return of materials to storage (Exh A 1(i)).
4. **Coil cleaning content** per unit type, done on the filter visit, plus water hook-up, chemical mixing and drain
   protection, with DEN-approved chemicals (Exh A 11(6)).
5. **Labor rates** at the Exhibit D prevailing wage and fringe for each role ("All rates must include Prevailing Wage Rates
   where required"): Building Engineer for specialists and supervisors and Laborer: Common or General for laborers
   [LUKE: HQ default names the classifications priced; DEN: Q8], above the City Minimum Wage (III-23), fully burdened with payroll taxes, workers' compensation and
   Colorado paid sick leave, and a night differential for the night crew.
6. **Fixed costs** spread over the Appendix II workload: two shift supervisors, a site manager, the filter transport vehicle
   and electric carts (Exh A 3(3)(c),(e)), HEPA vacuums, coil equipment, ladders, tablets, work tracking access, PPE, monthly
   safety training, badging, parking, filter logistics and inventory labor, used-filter disposal, and mobilization.
7. **Materials** per unit: coil cleaner, sheeting, bags for used filters, markers and HEPA bags. Filters are DEN's (Exh A
   1(c)).
8. **Management and fee**: the subcontractor's overhead and profit and Brown Bison's contract administration (monthly
   invoices with PO reports, DSBO and certified-payroll reporting, monthly reports) and fee.

The Pricing Form's unit counts and services per year are used exactly as DEN printed them [DEN: Q1]. Years 2-5 carry an
annual increase within the form's limit ("Total increase for years 2-5 must not exceed 5% per year") because prevailing
wage and fringe rates "adjust on the yearly anniversary" of the advertisement date (SC 6.C.i) [DEN: Q4 on Exh A 13(4)].

## Tab 3. Understanding the Project (p.23, IV-3.3)

### 3.1 Understanding of the Scope of Work

DEN needs a contractor to replace filters and clean coils on its HVAC fleet across "the Denver International Airport (DEN)
including outlying and other airport support buildings" (Exh A 1(b)), providing "all certified and experienced labor,
equipment, materials (not including filters) and expertise" (Exh A 1(c)). Work arrives as "monthly work orders" (Exh A
3(1)(a)(i)), "provided to the Contractor by DEN one month prior" in batches "intended to provide the contractor with a
month's worth of work" (Exh A 4(7)).

**The fleet and the workload.** Exhibit A 3(2) estimates 40 smoke control fans, 150 air curtains, 405 AHUs, 125 MAU/DOAS
units, 95 RTUs, 30 CRAC units, 200 ACUs, 430 FCUs and 225 fan-powered VAVs, plus 150 passenger loading bridge PCA units "not
anticipated at this time". Appendix II sets the frequencies: quarterly for smoke fans, AHUs, MAU/DOAS, RTUs, CRACs and FCUs;
every four months for air curtains; semi-annual for ACUs and FP-VAVs; bi-monthly filters and semi-annual coils for about 15
baggage-tunnel FCUs, for "Totals: 5774 filter changes; 1699 coil cleanings" a year. Appendix III lists 1,288 completed work
orders with about 8,400 filter pieces for one service of every listed asset, mostly 2-inch pleated filters, with 6-inch
final filters behind 2-inch pre-filters on many AHUs. By location the listed assets are in the Main Terminal (368),
Concourse B (201), Concourse C (176), Concourse A (136), the Airport Office Building (88), the hotel and transit center (57)
and a long tail of outlying buildings: maintenance center, WorldPort, fire stations, cargo buildings, the AGTS train tunnel,
the data center, toll plazas, antenna towers, lift stations and trailers.

The four counts differ (Exhibit A 3(2) about 1,700 units; Appendix II 1,684; the Pricing Form 1,795; Appendix III 1,288
rows). We priced the Pricing Form quantities exactly as printed and staffed to Appendix II, the schedule DEN calls "the basis
for minimum staffing expectations", and asked DEN which governs [DEN: Q1, Q2].

**What each visit includes** (Appendix I): unit shut down and LOTO through the DEN HVAC team; filters replaced and dated;
before and after photos; panels closed and clips secured; HEPA vacuuming of the "filter bank, frames, and immediate plenum
area"; coil inspection on every annual and semi-annual work order, with cleaning "at the discretion of the PM" and "prior to
installing new air filters"; "zero air bypass in accordance with NAFA guidelines"; documentation; and the surrounding area,
floor drains and mechanical room left "better than they were found".

**What we manage around the visits:** filter inventory "at DEN", staging in mechanical rooms, transport from WorldPort and
through the tunnel system, work with "DEN's provider of air filters" and disposal of used filters (Exh A 3(3)); on-site
supervision, coordination and safety oversight (3(1)(c)); deficiency reporting "immediately" (3(1)(f)); per-unit records in
an approved online work tracking program (4(1)); monthly reports, a monthly equipment inventory list and status meetings
(4(4)-(6)); monthly invoices by the 15th (5(1)); a safety plan with training records (11(1)); and full airport security
compliance (Section 12; SC 12-13; DEN Rules Part 20).

### 4.6 Filter inventory handling (DEN supplies the filters)

### 4.7 Work sequence at each unit (Appendix I)

1. Confirm the unit and asset number; request shutdown and apply LOTO through the DEN HVAC team. 2. Pre-service photo of the
filters and magnehelic reading where a gauge exists. 3. Remove filters, bag them at the unit. 4. HEPA-vacuum the filter bank,
frames and immediate plenum area. 5. On annual and semi-annual work orders, inspect the coil; clean it when the PM directs,
before new filters go in. 6. Install new filters dated with permanent marker, check zero bypass per NAFA guidance, close
panels and secure clips. 7. Post-service photo and magnehelic reading; restore the unit through the DEN HVAC team. 8. Clean
the area, floor drains and mechanical room; remove any food or drink waste. 9. Record the work order in the tracking program
before leaving the room. 10. Report any deficiency, failed component or hazard to the PM immediately (Exh A 3(1)(f)).

### 4.8 Coil cleaning method

DEN-approved, non-acid coil cleaners only, with SDSs approved before use (Exh A 11(6)). Protect electrical components and
the area; apply cleaner, allow dwell time, rinse at low pressure where drains allow; use no-rinse cleaners on CRAC units,
above-ceiling FCUs and FP-VAVs and anywhere water is unsafe; comb bent fins; confirm the drain pan and condensate line flow;
pre- and post-cleaning photos. RTU and ACU prices include the evaporator and condensing coils unless DEN limits the scope
[DEN: Q6]. Outdoor coil rinsing is scheduled April through October.

notes; anything else the PM asks for [DEN: Q16 whether DEN provides system access]. The technician enters the record before
