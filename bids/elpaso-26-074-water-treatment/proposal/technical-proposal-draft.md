**Example: the main CUP tower in the September report.** The report recommended to "turn up pump to get the chemical dosage

1. Retest conductivity and inhibitor residual to confirm the readings.

3. Check the conductivity sensor calibration and the bleed valve. A high-reading sensor or a leaking bleed valve lowers
   cycles and washes treated water out of the tower. Turning up the pump would then burn chemical without fixing the cause.
4. Check the biocide feed, because free chlorine read 0.00 ppm as Cl₂ on the same test.
5. Correct what is wrong, retest before leaving, and return within [FACT: number] days to confirm.
6. Report the readings against their limits and keep the item open until it holds in range.

its limit table (999 μS/cm against an 800 minimum) do not agree, and a clear range removes that doubt.
