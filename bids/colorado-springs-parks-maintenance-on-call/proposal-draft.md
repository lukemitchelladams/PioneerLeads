## 2.5.1 Technical area
### Understanding
PRCS manages 24,000+ acres, 326 miles of trail, 152 developed parks, three community centers, two cemeteries, and two golf courses, and uses this pool to get qualified contractors onto small and mid-size projects quickly. Work arrives as task orders with fixed unit pricing from Appendix A, capped at $500,000 ($200,000 PPRTA, $150,000 federal). Federally funded task orders carry Appendix C clauses, including prevailing wage, and PPRTA orders carry Appendix D. Work is on public park land in active use, so public safety, traffic control, and clean sites matter as much as the finished product.

Landscape construction (B.3.7): retaining-wall repair and construction (segmental block, timber, stone, cast-in-place), turf and planting repair and installation including soil prep, seeding, sodding, erosion control, and general landscape construction: rock and mulch beds, edging, hardscape, minor concrete, minor fencing, benches, picnic tables, signage. Janitorial (B.3.6): interior and exterior cleaning of park buildings on scheduled or as-needed task orders, contractor-supplied products meeting health and environmental standards. Striping (B.3.16): parking lot and trail marking, ADA stalls, fire lanes, arrows, crosswalks, stencils, removal and re-layout, with traffic control, to ADA, CDOT, and MUTCD.

### Approach
1. Task-order intake: within one business day of a task order, our project lead walks the site with PRCS staff, confirms scope, access, hours, and funding source (PPRTA/federal flags), and returns a written scope confirmation with unit-price quantities from Appendix A.
2. Schedule management: a simple written schedule per task order (start, milestones, completion) shared with the PRCS contact; progress reported at each milestone and weekly on anything over one week; changes flagged in writing before they happen.
3. Quality control: the Brown Bison lead inspects at start, mid-point, and completion against the scope confirmation; photos before, during, and after; punch list closed before invoicing; warranty terms per the contract on retaining walls, plantings, and striping.
4. Safety: OSHA-compliant crews, daily tailgate talks, PPE, traffic control plans for any work near roadways or parking (MUTCD), work zones fenced or coned in active parks, no equipment left unattended in public areas, City security requirements followed for building access.
5. Subcontracting: landscape construction performed by [Alex Ditto's company] under Brown Bison supervision; janitorial by [sub]; striping by [sub]. Brown Bison remains the single responsible party, carries the contract insurance, and names the City as additional insured.
6. Foreseeable issues and adjustments: weather windows for seeding, sod, and striping (we schedule around them and hold materials); historic retaining walls (we photograph and match materials before disturbing anything); event conflicts in parks (we coordinate dates with PRCS and shift to off-hours where needed); prevailing-wage task orders (we run certified payroll on those orders).
