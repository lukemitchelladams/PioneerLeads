3.3 SELECTION COMMITTEE 
 
A selection committee will review all proposals. Through this process, the City will determine 
which proposals are acceptable or unacceptable.  The City will notify, in writing, the Offerors 
whose proposals are deemed to be unacceptable. Those Offerors offering proposals deemed to 
be acceptable by the City will be evaluated by grouping and scored by grouping by the selection 
committee. This scoring will determine  which Offerors are considered to be in the competitive 
range and may be the basis for an award decision without further steps.  
 
If the selection committee elects not to award based upon evaluation scoring, it may engage in a 
forced elimination process. To inform this process, it may require oral presentations or interviews 
with the Offerors considered to be in the competitive range. If oral presentations or interviews are 
conducted, they may also be scored, or they may simply be considered as information supporting 
the forced elimination process. The selection committee may request revisions to the proposal 
from each of the Offerors at the conclusion of the interviews. The intent of the forced elimination 
process is to reach consensus. The decision will be based on all relevant factors and based upon 
perception of best value. The final decision may or may not exactly reflect scoring ranking. 
 
The City also reserves  the right to request best and final offers from all Offerors at any point in 
the proposal evaluation process.

APPENDIX B – SCOPE OF WORK 
 
B.1 BACKGROUND AND PURPOSE 
 
The Colorado Springs Parks, Recreation and Cultural Services (PRCS) Department stewards one 
of the most extensive and diverse municipal park systems in the region. PRCS manages more 
than 24,000 acres of parkland and open space, 326 miles of trails, 152 develop ed parks, three 
community centers, two cemeteries, two golf courses, and numerous cultural and tourist 
destinations. This expansive system plays a vital role in supporting the City’s health, vibrancy, 
and overall quality of life. 
 
To maintain, enhance, and expand these public assets, PRCS regularly undertakes a wide range 
of capital, maintenance, planning, and operational projects. The variety and volume of work 
requires access to reliable, qualified, and licensed contractors and co nsultants capable of 
providing services across multiple disciplines. 
 
Through this Request for Proposals (RFP), PRCS seeks to establish on call contracts with a pool 
of experienced firms who can assist with both major and minor projects as needs arise. The 
purpose of these on call contracts is to streamline project delivery,  ensure timely access to 
specialized expertise, and support the Department’s ongoing commitment to stewardship, safety, 
sustainability, and exceptional public service. 
 
B.2 SCOPE OF WORK 
 
The Contractor shall provide on-call services as defined in task order scopes for major and minor 
projects. Services will also include coordination and supervision of all elements of the project 
which includes but is not limited to the obtaining of permits and  licenses, bringing on 
subcontractors where applicable, and overseeing the work of those subcontractors for the 
duration of the project. The contractor shall manage schedules, budget, and materials for the 
project, and keep in close communication with the City’s project manager or representative.
